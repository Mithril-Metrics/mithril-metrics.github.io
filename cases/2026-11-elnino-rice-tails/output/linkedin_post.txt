This El Niño is among the strongest on record. In Southeast Asian rice data since 1961, the national damage arrives a year later than most people expect.

FAO and WFP have launched their first joint anticipatory action appeal for this event. So we asked: when do El Niño's rice losses show up, and how deep can they go?

What we found:
→ In the year El Niño begins, no detectable rise in shortfalls: 11% of country-years vs 15% in other years
→ The year after, rice production in Indonesia, the Philippines and Malaysia runs 4.9% below trend. No effect on the mainland
→ Harvests 10% or more below trend come 8.3 times as often as calm-year variation predicts

What it means: if you are timing early action or imports, watch the 2027 harvests in the maritime countries, and size buffers for the bad years, not the typical ones.

National averages hide local losses, so this is a floor, not a full picture.

Method: detrended FAOSTAT rice yields, 6 countries, 1961-2024; country fixed effects; intervals from resampling whole years
