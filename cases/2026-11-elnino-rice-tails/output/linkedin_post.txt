A strong to very strong El Niño is under way. In Southeast Asian rice data since 1961, onset years show no rise in national shortfalls of 5% or more; any signal came a year later.

FAO and WFP have launched a joint anticipatory action appeal for this event. So we asked: when do El Niño's rice losses show up, and how deep can they go?

What we found:
→ In the year El Niño begins, no detectable rise in shortfalls of 5% or more: 11% of country-years vs 15% in other years. Smaller losses cannot be ruled out
→ The year after, production in Indonesia, the Philippines and Malaysia runs about 4.1% below trend (exploratory). No detectable effect on the mainland
→ Deep shortfalls of 10% or more have come more often than typical years suggest, but 8 of 11 came before 1980

What it means: for timing imports or reserves, the national data point, tentatively, to the harvests after the El Niño year in the maritime countries, as agronomic studies for Indonesia predict.

National annual totals hide local losses and arrive late, so early action still needs seasonal, provincial data.

Method: detrended FAOSTAT rice data, 6 countries, 1961-2024; fixed effects, block bootstrap
