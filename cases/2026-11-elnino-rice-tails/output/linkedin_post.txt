A strong to very strong El Niño is under way. In Southeast Asian rice data since 1961, onset years show no rise in national shortfalls of 5% or more; any signal came a year later.

FAO and WFP have launched a joint anticipatory action appeal for this event. So we asked: when do the rice losses show up, and how deep?

What we found:
→ In the year El Niño begins, no detectable rise in shortfalls of 5% or more: 11% of country-years vs 15% otherwise. Smaller losses cannot be ruled out
→ The year after, production in Indonesia, the Philippines and Malaysia runs about 4.1% below trend (interval 1.4% to 5.8%; exploratory, mostly Indonesia). Nothing detectable on the mainland
→ Shortfalls of 10% or more are rare (11 of 365, 8 before 1980); whether that exceeds a bell curve depends on how spread is measured

What it means: for timing imports or reserves, the national data point, tentatively, to the harvests after the El Niño year in the maritime countries, consistent with agronomic studies for Indonesia. They are not anticipatory action triggers, which need seasonal, provincial data.

Method: detrended FAOSTAT rice data, 6 countries, 1961-2024; fixed effects, block bootstrap
