In April 2026 a tonne of urea cost 4.00 tonnes of maize on world markets, the most since at least 1990. What does that mean for harvests in Africa?

The OECD's new fertiliser brief and its 2026 policy report look at how countries cope with fertiliser shocks, but their support data cover only one Sub-Saharan African country. We looked at 14 maize-growing countries since 1990.

What we found:
→ In April 2026 a tonne of urea cost 4.00 tonnes of maize, the most since at least 1990
→ Years with a 10% higher urea-to-maize ratio have had 1.2% lower maize yields in the same season; with the ratio before planting, -0.7%, also clear
→ Where input subsidies are large, fertiliser use did not fall when prices rose (+3.4% vs -5.0%); the yield evidence is inconclusive

What it means: the world ratio is a rough guide to pressure on harvests, not a forecast, and farm-gate prices can tell a different story. Subsidy-heavy countries kept fertiliser use up; whether that protects harvests, our data cannot confirm.

Method: country trends and fixed effects, 14 countries, 1990-2024; World Bank Pink Sheet, FAOSTAT, FAO MAFAP
