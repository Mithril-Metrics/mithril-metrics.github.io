In April 2026 a tonne of urea cost 4.00 tonnes of maize on world markets, the most since at least 1990. What does that mean for harvests in Africa?

The OECD's new fertiliser work covers only one Sub-Saharan African country. We looked at 14 maize-growing countries since 1990.

What we found:
→ Years with a 10% higher urea-to-maize ratio have had 1.2% lower maize yields in the same season (-0.7% with the ratio before planting)
→ The link weakens once prices after planting are left out (previous year's ratio: -0.5%, not clear), so we read it as world market conditions as much as fertiliser costs
→ High-subsidy countries: recorded nitrogen use did not fall in high-price years (not on 2005-2024 alone); no consistent yield difference

What it means: the world ratio is a rough signal, not a forecast. World prices only; farm-gate ratios differ widely and were not used. These data cannot confirm that input subsidies protect harvests.

Method: country trends and fixed effects, 14 countries, 1990-2024; World Bank Pink Sheet, FAOSTAT, FAO MAFAP
